Direct value-code readout brings no overall performance advantage

Does predicting fixed value codes directly, while retaining NW plus a Unit-local correction and removing the output inverse, improve prediction?

Retrospective · Published 2026-10-24

Protocol

V8.4.2 migrates from V8.2 parent 304, then trains for 2,400 updates / 21.113 effective minutes. The first 1,200 episodes match historical same-parent V8.4.1 and V8.2 controls. Validation selects step 2,400 before full Small51 testing.

Results

Validation aggregate falls from 0.267899 to 0.181050. At matched 1,200 episodes V8.4.2 wins/ties/loses 23/2/26 against V8.4.1. The selected model reaches local Elo 910.2, rank 45/47, and loses 33 of 51 tables to the V8.2 1,200-step control. Continuation improves the old numeric mean but lowers median R² from 0.473334 to 0.399661.

Failure & lesson

Aligning output space and loss resolves one implementation choice without automatically fixing representation or generalization. Faster training and better aggregate validation are insufficient; retention costs beyond the mean matter.

Limits

The 2,400 vs 1,200 comparison is not dose-matched. The selected V8.4.1 reference also has a different initialization. Small51 is repeatedly used for development; Elo is not an official rank. The existing development42 worlds are not independent confirmation.

Decision at the time

Keep the endpoint, complete state and counterexamples. The candidate did not meet the adoption condition against V8.2; defaults are unchanged.
