Targeted training improves fit, not Query performance

Does allocating more exposure to eight tables lagging validation baselines beat uniform training?

Retrospective · Published 2026-10-24

Protocol

Two arms restore the same parent-304 weights, Adam and RNG state. Each runs 960 updates: 768 real and 192 old synthetic, totaling 19.745 effective minutes. Uniform: 16 exposures per real table. Targeted: 36 for eight focus tables and 12 for the other forty. Selection uses validation gaps before training.

Results

All eight focus tables improve training AUC against the parent, but only two improve on test. Against uniform, targeted validation/test AUC falls by 0.01705/0.00686. Local parent/uniform/targeted Elo is 999.5/958.8/940.3.

Failure & lesson

More focused exposure improves fitting without improving Query ranking here. Falling rank also does not erase regression gains: uniform test wins against XGBoost increase from 22 to 24 overall and from 4 to 6 for regression. Explain both metric and rank behavior.

Limits

A short run with a small validation set cannot identify overfitting, gradient conflict or capacity as the cause. The 51 tables are familiar development data. This rejects the tested allocation recipe, not all targeted curricula.

Decision at the time

Do not adopt the targeted recipe. Keep both arms and the regression gains; project defaults remain unchanged.
