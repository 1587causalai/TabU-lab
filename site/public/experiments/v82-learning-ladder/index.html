2026-10-13Gains & tradeoffs

New rules are learnable; shared learning still has retention costs

Can one model learn several new worlds, and how do ordering and replay affect transfer and retention?

Retrospective · Published 2026-10-24

Protocol

Stage one compares six separately adapted models (256 updates each) with one shared model consuming the same 1,536 target episodes. Stage two restores the same parent for two 3,072-update arms, with identical 50% replay; only target ordering differs: round-robin vs 16-step blocks. Total: 9,216 updates / 64.114 effective minutes.

Results

Separate threshold/regime regression adaptation reaches R² 0.8939/0.9327. Shared same-world confirmation is lower than separate adaptation on all six worlds. With replay, the round-robin single checkpoint reaches local Elo 999.5, rank 40/47; regression on 84 new worlds rises from 0.40433 to 0.45439, with classification-probability and old-classification costs.

Failure & lesson

Learning one task, learning many rules jointly and transferring to new worlds are different questions. Replay and interleaving support coexistence without eliminating tradeoffs. Block ordering improves mean new-world regression but fares worse on the real panel and several focal mechanisms.

Limits

New worlds are instances of existing mechanism families, not unseen mechanisms. Limited single-stream exploration. Local 999.5 ties an older two-model recipe at displayed precision; it is not an official rank or broad advance. Cross-stage gains cannot be assigned to replay alone.

Decision at the time

Retain round-robin plus replay as a comparison candidate. Investigate probability quality and retention; defaults are unchanged.
